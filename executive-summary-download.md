# Executive Summary

## Designing a GM-Style Roster and Financial Framework

This project presents a professional operating model for college football in the 2026 revenue-sharing era. The proposed framework centers on a general manager role responsible for balancing a 105-man roster limit, distributing a finite $22,000,000 internal revenue-sharing cap, and coordinating responsibly with third-party collective activity.

The framework argues that football programs now need a true front-office structure. The general manager serves as the central connector across coaches, players, compliance, collectives, and institutional leadership while managing roster construction, portal acquisition, retention planning, and financial allocation.

## Core Takeaways

- The operating environment has shifted from the decentralized NIL market of 2021 to a more structured revenue-sharing model.
- The GM role is best positioned to manage roster math, internal cap logic, and acquisition pathways.
- Internal rev-share should primarily support retention, while collective support should be aligned more strategically with acquisition.
- A 105-man roster and finite internal budget demand visual planning tools, multi-year projection models, and tiered player valuation.

## Project Foundation

This framework is built from SPAD 498 research, including dossier work, industry interviews, and state-of-the-industry analysis focused on the operational realities of modern college football administration.

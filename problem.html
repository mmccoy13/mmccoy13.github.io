The 2026 shift

The college football GM now operates inside a regulated revenue-sharing economy.

The problem is no longer just NIL chaos. It is the transition from the 2021 "Wild West" to a post-settlement environment where roster limits, internal payroll discipline, and outside-deal oversight all collide.

The catalyst

2021 vs. 2026

In 2021, NIL created a fast-moving, largely decentralized market. In 2026, the operating challenge is more structured: programs must balance internal revenue sharing, a 105-man roster ceiling, and new scrutiny around third-party collective activity.

Why it matters

Front-office pressure

The GM becomes the person responsible for translating market pressure into a legal, repeatable, competitive system.

Operational challenges grid

The new pressure points

Portal churn vs. 105-man limits

Every portal addition now has sharper opportunity cost because a roster spot and internal dollars are both constrained.

CSC compliance friction

Outside activity requires cleaner process, documentation, and role separation than the early NIL period demanded.

In-house payroll vs. booster inducements

Programs need a clear logic for retention spending internally while coordinating acquisition support responsibly through third-party pathways.
